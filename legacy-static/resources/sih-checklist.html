Smart India Hackathon Prep Checklist

Tick these off stage by stage. Rules and dates change every year, so always confirm the current guidelines on the official SIH portal.

R/refactorslife.in

1 · Build the team

  • Read this year's official guidelines and timeline before anything else
  • Meet the team size and composition rules (check the current rules, e.g. the female-member requirement)
  • Cover the skills: frontend, backend, ML/data, design and one strong presenter
  • Get a faculty mentor who'll actually review your work
  • Agree on roles, a group chat and a weekly meeting time

2 · Pick the problem statement

  • Shortlist 3–5 statements the whole team understands
  • Write the core problem in one sentence: who suffers, and how
  • Check feasibility: can you build a working demo in the time you have?
  • Weigh competition: popular statements attract far more teams
  • Research what already exists, and how yours is different

3 · Idea submission & internal round

  • Use the official PPT template and stay within its slide limits
  • Cover: problem, solution, uniqueness, feasibility, impact
  • Add a clear architecture diagram and the tech stack
  • Show mockups or screenshots, not just text
  • Clear your college internal hackathon (nominations usually come from here)

4 · Build the prototype

  • Make one core flow work end-to-end before adding features
  • Use Git & GitHub from day one, with one branch per feature
  • Keep sample data ready so the demo never depends on luck
  • Record a backup demo video in case the Wi-Fi or the server fails
  • Write a clean README with setup steps and screenshots

5 · Grand finale

  • Rehearse the pitch to fit the time you're given, then rehearse again
  • Every member can explain the whole project, not just their part
  • Prepare answers on scalability, cost, data privacy and security
  • Act on mentor/judge feedback between rounds, and show that you did
  • Sleep in shifts. A tired team gives a weak final demo

6 · After the hackathon // win or lose

  • Make the repo public with a polished README
  • Post the journey on LinkedIn and tag your teammates and mentor
  • Add the project to your resume with measurable results
  • Write down what you'd do differently for next year
